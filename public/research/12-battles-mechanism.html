1. What a battle is

  • N seats (2 or more) and R rounds. Each round names one box; in that round every seat opens that box once.
  • Entry per seat = the sum of the round prices, plus the admin's fee if one is set. It is held from the player's credit when the seat is taken and returned if the battle is cancelled or never fills.
  • What the seats pull makes the pot. The mode decides who takes it.
  • A battle does not change any box's odds. It changes who takes the items and how much is bet at once.

2. One battle, step by step

StepWhat happensWhy it is done that way
1Someone creates a battle: a mode, a seat count and one box per round, all from the lists the admin allowed.The player chooses inside the admin's limits. Nothing about odds or boxes is decided by the player.
2Before anyone else joins, the server draws one secret for the whole battle and shows its hash on the page.Every roll comes from that secret. Showing the hash first proves it was fixed before anyone paid or sat down.
3When a seat is taken, the server gives it a random seed of its own and records it. The entry is held from the player's credit. Players never type a seed.Each seat's seed is stored before the start, so it cannot be changed afterwards. The hold means a seat is never taken with money that is then spent elsewhere.
4When the last seat is taken, a short countdown runs on the server and the battle starts. From here nothing that feeds a roll can change.The server owns the clock; no client can start or delay a battle.
5Every seat opens the round's box. Each open is a normal open: secret + the seat's seed + a spin number → a roll between 0 and 1 → read against the box's odds table.Battles reuse the single-open rule, so one check covers both. The spin number is the pull's position: round × seats + seat + 1.
6After the last round the mode decides who takes the pot. If seats tie, one extra roll breaks the tie.The extra roll uses the next spin number and every seat's seed joined together, so no single seat controls it.
7The winner's items become awards (ship or trade in) and the credit goes to the ledger. Then the battle is marked settled and the secret is published.Money moves first, status last, so a crash can never leave a battle finished but unpaid.
8Anyone can check the battle: recompute every roll from the secret, the seeds and the odds tables.The check proves the secret matches the hash, every pull landed where the page says, and the right seat won.

3. The modes

ModeWho winsWhat is paid
NormalHighest total over all roundsEvery item and credit pulled by every seat, to the winner
CrazyLowest totalSame
TerminalHighest pull in the last roundSame
JackpotDrawn in proportion to the totals, with the extra rollSame
GroupEveryoneThe pot split equally; odd cents to seat 0
BreakEveryoneEach seat keeps its own pulls; no pot
CoinflipOne fair extra roll; no boxesThe two stakes less the disclosed edge, to the winner

Ties in normal, crazy and terminal are broken by one extra roll among the tied seats, in seat order. Jackpot with every total at zero falls back to a uniform draw.

4. The status of a battle

Every battle is a row in the database with a status. Screens show plain words; the codes stay in the API. A status only ever changes by one guarded step: the server writes the new status together with a version number and refuses the write if anyone else moved first.

On screenCodeWhat it meansWhat the database holds
WaitingopenCreated; seats free; credit held for the seats takenThe secret hash, the mode, the seats, the rounds with their table hashes, the holds
Waiting · startingfullEvery seat taken; the countdown runs on the serverThe seats with their seeds, fixed; the start time
Ongoingin_progressThe server resolves every round from the committed inputs and writes the pullsEvery pull with its spin number, digest, roll, outcome and value
Ongoing · settlingsettlingAwards and ledger entries are being writtenThe pot, the winners, the shares; item awards and ledger rows
SettledfinishedEverything is paid; the secret is published; anyone can checkThe secret, the settled time, the result; never edited again
CancelledcancelledCreator (while alone) or admin cancelled before the startThe reason, the refunds
ExpiredexpiredNever filled within the admin's windowThe refunds

The only paths: open → full → in_progress → settling → finished; full → open (a seat left); open or full → cancelled; open → expired. Because every roll is computed from fixed inputs, a server that crashes halfway runs the same resolution again and gets the same pulls: nothing is re-rolled and nothing is paid twice.

5. The sums

  • Every seat plays the same table with the same odds, so over many battles each seat takes home what its own boxes pay back on average. A battle changes the shape of the result (all or nothing) and the size of the bet (every round at once), not the average.
  • What the house keeps is the boxes' margin, plus a disclosed fee if the admin sets one. The fee is added on top of the entry and never touches the pot.
  • Bots do not change a player's expectation. When the house fills the other seats with bots it funds those seats itself; against the one real player it keeps exactly what one open of each box would keep.
  • The default Evo Verse battle (4 seats, 3 rounds of the vendor box at $50, entry $150) is worked through with live numbers at /docs/battles, section 5.

6. Bots

  • Off unless the admin switches them on. When on, the creator of a waiting battle that nobody joins can ask for bots; the server fills every free seat with a bot from the admin's list and the countdown starts.
  • A bot is a name and a picture, nothing more. Same boxes, same odds, same committed secret; the server gives it a seed like any seat; it holds no credit; what it wins returns to the house with no award and no ledger row.
  • Labelled as a bot on every screen, in the lobby and in the game history.
  • A bot with better odds than players would be a rig. The engine has no setting for it and the kit forbids it. Evocase described its bots the same way in its 15 October demo: "just names and images, no higher configuration".

7. The lobby

A battle is a room. Creating one opens a room in the lobby, where everyone sees the mode, the boxes, the entry, the seats taken and free, and the secret hash. Other players press Join to take a free seat, in any position; when every seat is taken the room starts. Before the start a player can leave and get the entry back. If nobody comes, the creator can ask bots to fill the rest, when the admin allows bots. Ongoing and settled rooms stay visible for a moment with the winner and the pot. The Evo Verse Battles tab shows this lobby with simulated players.

8. The check

After a battle is settled the page shows the secret, every seat's seed, the table hash of every round and the spin number of every pull. The check recomputes every roll and compares four things: the secret hashes to what was shown before the battle; every pull lands where the page says; every total matches; the same seat wins. The public verify endpoint does the same on the server for anyone with the battle id.

10. What the player sees

  • A lobby: open battles with mode, seats taken, entry, boxes and the secret hash; Create offers only what the admin allowed; Join; private battles by code.
  • The battle room: seats with names (bots labelled), the boxes per round, the secret hash, the countdown from the server, then the rounds playing back from the server's pull records, totals updating, the winner, the pot, what each seat takes home, Ship or Trade in for item awards.
  • The result and the check: the secret revealed, the seeds, the spin numbers, the table hashes, a Check this battle button.
  • Nothing is computed in the browser before the server has written it.

11. History and reports

  • A player's game history lists every battle they sat in with their pulls, the result and what they took home, next to their single opens. The admin's player screen shows "Boxes opened" and "Battles played".
  • The admin dashboard shows battles played per day and per mode, and the gross gaming revenue per game mode: what players paid in minus what players took home. Bot seats are not player money.
  • A settled battle is never edited.

12. Where this comes from

  • The mechanic and the proof are the Misty single-open recipe (document 06) extended with one secret per battle, a seed per seat, a nonce fixed by position and one extra roll.
  • The product facts (bots as names and images a player can ask to join, "ongoing" and "settled", game history, GGR by game mode) are from the Evocase demo of 15 October 2026.
  • The Evo Verse battles engine that the simulator used until 19 October was a research tool: one shared random stream for every seat, a favoured-bots rig, a random tie-break, no database. It stays available on the Cases.gg pages for rigging research; it is not the production logic and is not in the kit.

13. Critique

  • Battles multiply the bet. One click wagers every round at once; the vendor's own figure was that battles make 90% of all wagering. The fairness of each roll is unchanged; the amount at risk per minute is not.
  • Bots mean the house sits across the table. The odds are the same, so the player's expectation equals a single open, but a bot winning the pot is the house collecting the player's pulls as well as its margin. The label on the seat is what keeps this honest.
  • The check proves the roll, not the value. Items count at listed value; what they fetch when sold is a separate question.
