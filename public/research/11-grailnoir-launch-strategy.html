In short

  • A mystery box business can work: IcyBox reached hundreds of thousands of downloads in six months, Jemlit runs 348 boxes, Pop Mart made RMB 37 billion in 2025. The walls are known: Stripe, TikTok Shop, eBay and state law set what a box may do.
  • A generous launch is affordable. At honest values with a 90% cash buyback, a 95% box keeps about $15 per $100 before fees. Sell the first box at cost, one per account, labelled. Cost: $7 to $15 per first paying customer.
  • No dummy users. Legally it is fraud on partners (Javice: 85 months, $300 million) and deception of players (FTC, DMCC, EU blacklist). Commercially, partners measure sell-through, repeat rate and chargebacks, not user counts.
  • Supply before customers: two or three dealers on consignment at the trade's terms. Not an open marketplace.
  • License the engine last, after a year of public, checkable draws.
  • Each phase opens on a number, not a date. Lean phase 1: $35,000 to $50,000, 10 to 14 weeks. Standard: $90,000 to $130,000. To the engine gate: $135,000 to $210,000 lean, $260,000 to $425,000 standard, about 18 months.

2. The generous launch

2.1 What odds cost

Assumptions, editable in the simulator: 80% of wins sold back; cash buyback 90% of value; kept items sourced at 85% of market; credit costs 55 cents per dollar of face; fees, chargebacks and cash-out take 6 to 8 points.

Payout ruleCost per $100 at return RR = 85%R = 95%R = 100%
Cash buyback at 90%0.89 Rmargin $24margin $15margin $11
Cash buyback at 100%0.97 Rmargin $18margin $8margin $3
Credit-only payout0.61 Rmargin $48margin $42margin $39
  • Inflating listed values barely helps a cash-buyback box: the buyback is paid on the inflated figure too. It mostly makes the odds screen look generous.
  • The house edge in a cash-buyback box is the gap to 100% plus the buyback haircut.
  • The loss case is a 100% cash trade-in at a return above 95%.

3.3 Honest ways to look bigger

  • Waitlist with a referral queue. Robinhood: about one million pre-launch. Median landing page: 11% of visitors sign up; referral rate 2 to 3% average, 15 to 20% excellent; about half convert within 30 days of launch, single digits after 90. Open within a month of closing the list.
  • Creator reveals, with the market value shown next to the operator value in the clip.
  • A public live feed of real opens, opt-in.
  • Letters of intent from two or three dealers.
  • The simulator: a dealer loads their stock and sees 200,000 opens.

4. Consignment supply

4.1 Why supply first

  • Rebag and Fashionphile bought stock outright, then added consignment to unlock idle inventory. Rebag's chief executive: "Luxury resale's biggest headache has always been on the supply side."
  • IcyBox does not own its watches; its terms give the buyer no interest in an item until in possession.
  • Bezel authenticates in-house; in 2024, 24% of watches submitted failed.

4.3 Settle before a dealer joins

  1. GrailNoir sets the odds from market value; the dealer sees them before agreeing.
  2. An authentication partner on GrailNoir's account; the seller is liable for a failed item.
  3. GrailNoir pays the buyback from margin; the dealer is paid on the win, never on the resale.
  4. Insured shipping on GrailNoir's label; payment within days; 6 months with a 30-day exit; sell-through reported.

4.4 Not an open marketplace

  • Cold start on both sides; the resale platforms that won bought or consigned supply first.
  • A paid chance at a third-party prize is what Stripe prohibits and eBay bans; a box of consigned, authenticated, valued stock is a retail product with a disclosed surprise.
  • Seller-ships puts the delivery promise with the least accountable party; IcyBox's worst reviews are wrong-watch deliveries.

5. The engine, last

  • The backend kit (contract, test vectors, public verifier) is built and ported by the front-end team; the simulator is the back office.
  • Two ways to sell it: a merchant app at $19 to $149 a month plus 2%, or an operator licence at €20,000 to €50,000 setup plus 5 to 10% of revenue.
  • PackOS launched the comparable white-label product in September 2026 with custom pricing.
  • A year of public operation adds what no demo has: a log of checkable draws, a chargeback history, a dealer roster, a per-market compliance rule set.

Glossary

Return at listed values
Value of every outcome times its chance, divided by the price; the simulator calls it RTP.
Buyback
The operator's offer to take a won item back for cash or credit; rate and window are disclosed policy choices.
Consignment
Stock held and sold on a dealer's behalf; the dealer is paid an agreed share on sale and keeps title until then.
Welcome box
A first box sold at cost, labelled, one per account; accounted as acquisition cost.
Bonus abuse
Using promotions in ways the operator did not intend to extract positive expected value; the largest fraud category in iGaming.
Traction sheet
The monthly set of real numbers partners ask for instead of a user count.
